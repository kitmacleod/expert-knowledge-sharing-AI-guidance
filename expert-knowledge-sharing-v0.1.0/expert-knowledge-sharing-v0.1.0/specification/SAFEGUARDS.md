# Safeguards

Version: 0.1.0

These safeguards explain the policy behind the condensed requirements embedded directly in `RUNTIME-INSTRUCTIONS.md`.

## Voluntary participation

Knowledge-sharing sessions should be opt-in. Participants should understand the purpose, intended use, likely storage/access arrangements and their ability to stop or skip questions.

## Participant review

AI-generated knowledge notes are drafts. Participants should be able to correct substantive errors before a note is treated as reviewed knowledge.

## Attribution and provenance

Do not transform a person's account into anonymous organisational fact. Retain an appropriate date and provenance when the note is retained.

## No expert simulation

A collection represents things a person actually discussed. It must not be used to claim what that person would think, decide or recommend in situations they did not discuss.

## Secrets and security

Do not deliberately capture passwords, credentials, private keys, tokens or other secrets. Do not elicit exploit-enabling detail merely because it may be “knowledge”. Sensitive operational material should remain within the organisation's approved secure processes.

## Personal information

Capture organisationally useful knowledge, not unnecessary personal information or personal judgements about colleagues.

## Uncertainty

Fluent AI prose must not convert uncertain recollection into apparent fact. Preserve material qualifications and source differences.

## Appropriate environment

Use an organisation-approved AI environment appropriate to the information being discussed, together with the organisation's normal information-governance, security, retention and access controls. Product branding alone is not a substitute for those controls.

## Knowledge expires

Technical, scientific and organisational knowledge changes. A dated account can remain historically useful without remaining operationally correct.

## No completeness claim

A set of notes is a collection of useful slices of experience, not a complete representation of an individual's knowledge.

## Emotional and organisational context

Do not frame the interaction as extracting a departing person's brain. Treat contributors respectfully, particularly when knowledge transfer happens around role changes, retirement or organisational restructuring.
